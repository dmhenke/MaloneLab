Research Focus

Two Pillars of Inquiry

We pursue fundamental clinical questions in nephrology by coupling state-of-the-art single-cell and spatial technologies with rigorous computational analysis applied directly to human specimens. All bioinformatic pipelines are executed in-house.

01

Kidney Transplant Genetics & Rejection

We interrogate the transcriptional and molecular landscape of antibody-mediated and T-cell-mediated rejection in kidney allografts at single-cell resolution. Mapping distinct immune cell states, endothelial activation programmes, and novel disease markers within human biopsy specimens, our work aims to redefine the diagnostic and mechanistic understanding of rejection.

Active projects include the impact of loss of chromosome Y on delayed graft function — a transatlantic collaboration spanning UCD, Washington University, and Wake Forest — and the KOAR study evaluating donor-derived cell-free DNA (dd-cfDNA) as a non-invasive allograft surveillance biomarker.

02

Glomerular Diseases — FSGS, IgA Nephropathy & Alport Syndrome

We study the genetic architecture and molecular pathogenesis of nephrotic syndromes, with a focus on focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) and IgA nephropathy. Longstanding interests in inherited kidney diseases have yielded contributions to gene discovery in familial FSGS and functional variant validation in Alport syndrome (COL4A3, COL4A4, COL4A5).

We couple whole-exome sequencing, single-cell approaches, and computational genomics to uncover disease mechanisms, identify novel diagnostic markers, and characterise recurrent FSGS following renal transplantation — bridging both pillars of the lab's research programme.

Experimental Platform

Integrated Multiomics — All In-House

We integrate multiple single-cell and spatial technologies with end-to-end computational analyses performed entirely within the lab, enabling rapid iteration from bench specimen to biological insight.

Transcriptomics

scRNA-seq

Single-cell RNA sequencing of kidney transplant biopsy cores and glomerular disease specimens to resolve cell type-specific transcriptional programmes at single-cell resolution.

Epigenomics

scATAC-seq

Single-cell chromatin accessibility profiling to map regulatory element activity and transcription factor binding landscapes across cell types in diseased kidney tissue.

Multimodal

CITE-seq

Simultaneous measurement of transcriptome and surface proteome from the same single cells, enabling high-resolution immune cell phenotyping beyond mRNA alone.

Spatial Imaging

CODEX

Highly multiplexed single-cell spatial imaging to resolve the tissue architecture and spatial organisation of immune infiltrates within kidney biopsy sections in situ.

Computational

In-House Bioinformatics

End-to-end pipelines for clustering, trajectory analysis, cell–cell communication modelling, and expressed SNV analysis. All computational work performed internally.

In Vivo

Mouse Transplant Models

Murine kidney transplant rejection models complement human biopsy studies, enabling mechanistic validation of candidate pathways and potential therapeutic targets.

Principal Investigator

Dr. Andrew F. Malone

Dr. Andrew Malone is a Consultant Nephrologist and Physician-Scientist at St. Vincent's University Hospital and St. Vincent's Private Hospital, Dublin, where he leads a research laboratory at University College Dublin. He holds a Bachelor's degree in Biochemistry from Trinity College Dublin and his medical degree (MB BCh BAO) from the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland, followed by an MD by research at University College Dublin.

After completing Internal Medicine training at Beaumont Hospital Dublin, Dr. Malone undertook a three-year Nephrology Fellowship at Duke University Medical Center before completing a Transplantation Nephrology Fellowship at Washington University in St. Louis. He joined the faculty at Washington University School of Medicine in 2015 as Assistant Professor, subsequently promoted to Associate Professor of Medicine — a position he held for approximately a decade before returning to Ireland.

As a scientist, Dr. Malone has reached the forefront of transplantation genetics. His group was the first in the world to publish on the application of single-cell RNA-sequencing to a human kidney transplant biopsy core, a landmark study that has shaped the field's approach to allograft biology. His broader research spans the molecular mechanisms of FSGS, IgA nephropathy, and Alport syndrome, with contributions to gene discovery and functional variant classification.

Dr. Malone received the ASCI Young Physician-Scientist Award (2020), the CSCTR Early Career Development Award (2021), and served as a JASN Editorial Fellow (2021–2023). He was a member of the ASN Policy and Advocacy Committee and participated in the 2022 Kidney Health Advocacy Day on Capitol Hill. He is a Fellow of the Royal College of Physicians of Ireland (FRCPI) and Fellow of the American Society of Nephrology (FASN), listed on the Irish specialist register for Internal Medicine and Nephrology. He has served as Principal Site Investigator on numerous clinical trials in nephrology and transplantation since 2014.

Dr. Malone is an active contributor to nephrology education and discourse through the AJKD Blog, Renal Fellow Network (faculty advisor), NephMadness, and the nephrology community on social media as @AndrewFMalone.

Research Infrastructure

Kidney Biobanks

The Malone Lab maintains access to two carefully curated biobanks of kidney biopsy specimens paired with comprehensive clinical metadata — forming the substrate for translational multiomics research at scale.

Active · Dublin, Ireland

UCD-SVUH Kidney Biobank

Established following Dr. Malone's return to Ireland in 2024, the UCD–St. Vincent's University Hospital Kidney Biobank is a prospectively curated repository of kidney transplant and glomerular disease biopsy specimens paired with longitudinal clinical data, supporting multiomics investigations including scRNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and immune cell profiling.

Legacy · Washington University, St. Louis

WU Kidney Transplant Biobank

Built during Dr. Malone's decade at Washington University — home to one of the largest kidney transplant programmes in the United States — this repository of clinically indicated transplant biopsy specimens and associated metadata formed the substrate for the landmark 2018 scRNA-seq study and continues to support active transatlantic collaborations.
